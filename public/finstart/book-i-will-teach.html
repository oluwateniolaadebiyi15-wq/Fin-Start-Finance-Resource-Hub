I Will Teach You to Be Rich

by Ramit Sethi

2009 352 pages Beginner Personal Finance

Overview

Ramit Sethi wrote this book for people in their 20s and 30s who want a real, no-fluff system for managing money. The title is deliberately provocative — Sethi argues that "being rich" means living the life you want, not just accumulating a big number. The book is conversational, funny, and unapologetically direct. It won't ask you to cut out your daily coffee. Instead, it helps you build automated systems so your finances run in the background while you live your life.

The book covers six weeks of action: optimising credit cards, setting up the right bank accounts, opening investment accounts, automating transfers, and picking low-cost index funds. Sethi calls this "conscious spending" — spending freely on what you love by cutting mercilessly on what you don't care about.

Key Takeaways

1

Automate your finances: set up automatic transfers to savings and investments so the money moves before you can spend it.

2

Use credit cards strategically — they build credit history, offer rewards, and are safer than debit if you pay the full balance monthly.

3

Open a high-yield savings account and a Roth IRA (or your country's equivalent tax-advantaged account) immediately, even with small amounts.

4

"Conscious spending" means giving yourself full permission to spend on things you genuinely love — as long as you've already handled savings and investments.

5

Invest in low-cost index funds through your employer's retirement plan first, then a Roth IRA, then a taxable brokerage. Keep it boring and automatic.

"The single most important thing you can do for your finances is to spend extravagantly on the things you love, as long as you cut costs mercilessly on the things you don't."
— Ramit Sethi
