📚 Beginner Reading List

Resource Library

Six hand-picked books that make money management approachable — no economics degree required. Click any book to read a full summary.

Start With Any of These Six

Each one is written for everyday people, not finance professionals. Click a book to read the full summary, key takeaways, and notable quotes.

I Will Teach You to Be Rich
Ramit Sethi

I Will Teach You to Be Rich

A no-guilt, actionable 6-week plan that covers automating savings, picking the right accounts, and investing on autopilot — written for people in their 20s and 30s.

🟢 Best for: Total Beginners Read summary →
The Total Money Makeover
Dave Ramsey

The Total Money Makeover

A step-by-step "Baby Steps" plan to pay off debt and build wealth — starting with a $1,000 emergency fund. Very structured and motivating for debt payoff journeys.

🟢 Best for: Paying Off Debt Read summary →
Rich Dad Poor Dad
Robert T. Kiyosaki

Rich Dad Poor Dad

A classic that challenges traditional ideas about working for money versus making money work for you. Uses story-form to explain assets, liabilities, and financial mindset.

🟡 Best for: Mindset Shift Read summary →
The Automatic Millionaire
David Bach

The Automatic Millionaire

Introduces the "Latte Factor" — how small daily expenses add up — and a system for automating every financial goal so you never have to rely on willpower to save again.

🟢 Best for: Building Habits Read summary →
Your Money or Your Life
Vicki Robin & Joe Dominguez

Your Money or Your Life

A deeper look at the relationship between time, money, and life satisfaction. Introduces the concept of "life energy" — how many hours of your life you trade for each purchase.

🟡 Best for: Values & Purpose Read summary →
The Psychology of Money
Morgan Housel

The Psychology of Money

19 short stories about how people think about wealth, greed, and happiness. Explains why smart people make bad money decisions — and what to do instead.

🟡 Best for: Thinking Smarter Read summary →

Where to Get These Books for Free

Most of these titles are available at your local public library — including digital copies through apps like Libby or OverDrive. Many are also available as free audiobooks on YouTube or as summaries on platforms like Blinkist. You don't need to spend money to start learning about money.
