Rich Dad Poor Dad

by Robert T. Kiyosaki

1997 336 pages Mindset Classic

Overview

Rich Dad Poor Dad is one of the best-selling personal finance books of all time. Kiyosaki tells the story of two father figures in his life: his own father (the "Poor Dad") — a highly educated, salaried government employee who struggled financially — and his best friend's father (the "Rich Dad") — a self-made businessman who taught him to think differently about money. The contrast drives home the book's central message: financial education, not academic education, determines wealth.

The book challenges the traditional advice of "go to school, get a good job, save money." Instead, Kiyosaki argues that the wealthy build assets that generate income, while the middle class spends their earnings on liabilities they think are assets. It's best read as a mindset-opener rather than a practical instruction manual — the concepts are powerful but the specific advice is often vague.

Key Takeaways

1

The rich don't work for money — they make money work for them through assets like real estate, businesses, and investments.

2

Know the difference between assets (things that put money in your pocket) and liabilities (things that take money out). A house you live in is often a liability, not an asset.

3

Financial literacy is more valuable than academic achievement. Understanding income statements and balance sheets is a life skill schools rarely teach.

4

The "Rat Race" — working harder to earn more, then spending more — keeps most people from building wealth. Escape it by building passive income streams.

5

Fear and cynicism are the two biggest obstacles to financial success. Most people talk themselves out of opportunities before they start.

"The poor and the middle class work for money. The rich have money work for them."
— Robert T. Kiyosaki
